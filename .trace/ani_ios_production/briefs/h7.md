# Helper brief H7 — performance: the commit within budget

You are helper H7 on the ani layout engine. The orchestrator reviews every line and makes the
final, quiet-machine measurement. The Chief Justice judges the checkpoint.

## Why this slice exists

The P1 tribunal (P1-N001) requires the existing suites to be green, and `layout:COST` in
`tests/browser/cases/layout.ts` is red. The P2 work (measured capture, exact support check,
measured keep test) made the commit much slower. H5's profile of a 1,000-participant commit in
Chromium, means over 15 rounds, in ms:

| Scenario | capture | `engineState` at capture | raw reads (capture / flush) | flush |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 300 moving, at rest | 6.9 | 0.02 | 3.24 / 3.53 | 18.7 |
| 1,000 moving, at rest | 6.3 | 0.01 | 3.46 / 3.85 | 29.5 |
| 300 moving, tracks running | 10.7 | 3.59 | 3.23 / 3.64 | 37.8 |
| 1,000 moving, tracks running | 7.8 | 2.45 | 2.55 / 3.07 | 30.4 |

The P1 base whole commit was about 7 ms.

## Prerequisite 1 — authority

`packages/layout/ARCHITECTURE.md`:
- §11.5 cost gates and protocol: commit (capture + flush) ≤ 4 ms at 1,000 participants,
  unscrolled and scrolled; steady frame p95 ≤ 2 ms at 300 nested movers; exact read counts
  (≤ 2N rects and 2N layout-size pairs plus the computed values of §4.1 and §4.3; steady frames
  read zero);
- §4.1–§4.4 and §5.7: what must be read and computed;
- §7.1 `stats`.

## Prerequisite 2 — essential purpose

"Snap, never wrong motion". Performance work must not change a single drawn frame or report. Every
optimization is behaviour-preserving, proven by the full unit suite and the browser rows.

## Prerequisite 3 — task boundary

**Thesis.**
1. **Profile first.** Use a real CPU profiler: Playwright's CDP session with `Profiler.start` and
   `Profiler.stop` in Chromium, in a throwaway script that is not committed. Profile a
   1,000-participant commit in the COST row's four shapes. Attribute self and total time per
   function, and record the top 15 in your report.
2. **Remove the cost** that the authority does not require. Likely candidates:
   - per-node allocations;
   - repeated chain walks;
   - `engineState()` solving the whole frame at capture (only driven nodes need a pose and its
     rate at `t_k`);
   - `plannedBoxes`;
   - maps keyed by objects that could be arrays indexed by order;
   - closures in hot loops;
   - redundant `getComputedStyle` calls;
   - repeated string formatting.

   Keep `measure.ts` pure. Keep every read the authority requires, and never read after a write.
3. **Targets**, in Chromium and WebKit, with the §11.5 statistic on this container:
   - the legacy `layout:COST` row passes in Chromium, Firefox and WebKit, with its budget
     unchanged;
   - the whole commit at 1,000 participants is at or under 4 ms in Chromium and WebKit;
   - the steady frame p95 is at most 2 ms.

   If a target cannot be met without breaking the authority, stop at the best behaviour-preserving
   result. Report the remaining cost by function, with the authority sentence that requires it.

**Antithesis.**
- No change to drawn frames, reports, statuses or read semantics.
- No budget or tolerance change, no caching across a capture and its flush, no skipped required
  read, and no edit to `ARCHITECTURE.md`.
- No test edits except new performance tests.
- Do not push and do not touch a remote. Commit in your worktree with plain messages.

**Synthesis.**
- Before and after numbers per scenario and engine: the median of 5 fresh-process runs after 1
  warm-up, as in §11.5.
- The profile tables.
- Unit, typecheck and biome all clean.
- `layout:*`, `layout_meas:*`, `layout_spring:*`, `showcase:*`, `regressions:*` and
  `react_layout:*` unchanged in all three engines.

  Run timing measurements only when no other helper is running a browser: check `pgrep -f
  headless_shell` and `pgrep -f run.ts` first, and say in the report whether the machine was
  quiet.
